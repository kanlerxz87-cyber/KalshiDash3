The high-ROI model — one definition, two runtimes, measured out-of-sample

The brief asks for a second alert feed filtered to “events our model likes as high ROI”. This page is that model, in the open: the parameters below are read from data/roi_model.json at run time (the same file the code reads, so this text cannot drift away from the arithmetic), and the hit rate is measured on markets in the demo environment that have already settled. It is a modelled edge after fees, not a forecast, and it is not advice.

Read first. This ranks markets by the return on capital the current order book implies once you cross the spread and pay Kalshi's published taker fee. If the book is already right, the "edge" is an artefact of how the quote is blended, and you will have paid the spread and the fee to find that out. The measured numbers below are from demo data with mock funds, which Kalshi states may not reflect real markets (demo environment).

A quote pinned at $0.00 or $1.00 is left unpriced, on purpose

    
The demo book pins whole series at the edge of the price band. Measured, not assumed:
      127 of the 387 open markets in scope in data/live/markets.json (collected
      2026-11-20T15:19:53Z) carry yes_ask_dollars = 0.0000, and the band
      allows a bid at 1.0000. At a cost of exactly $0.00 the ratio (win − cost) / cost has no value: it is a division by
      zero in the scheduled scan, and Infinity in the browser, which would rank a pinned quote as
      the best edge on the board. Both are wrong, so the number is never produced. Both implementations drop such a side and report the market as
      unpriced instead, with the reason stated in its row.
      scripts/tests/test_roi_scan_snapshot.py re-runs the whole scan over that snapshot on every
      build, and cases 16–17 of scripts/tests/fixtures_roi_parity.json pin browser/CI agreement on
      the branch (the second case is the model's mirror and is labelled as constructed in the fixture).

What this model is not

  

    
Claim someone might read into itWhy that is wrongSource / guard

    
      
“A pick is expected to profit.”
          A pick is a priced deviation from the current book after a documented fee. It carries no probability of
              being right beyond the blend of mid, microprice and last that produced it.
          data/roi_model.json → scoring

      
“The hit rate generalises.”
          It is measured on a handful of recently settled markets in the demo environment, with a reduced feature
              set, and only for as long as the historical cutoff leaves them readable.
          historical data

      
“The links next to a jump are the news that caused it.”
          They are URLs copied out of that market's own record (rules / subtitle text). GET /markets has no news
              field, so causality is never asserted and the cell is blank when no URL is present.
          Get Markets response schema

      
“The model can be traded automatically.”
          Order placement needs an authenticated demo account and signed requests; nothing on this site holds a
              key, and nothing here posts an order. See
              Portfolio & trading for what is wired and what is not.
          API keys

      
“Browser and CI might be scoring differently.”
          Both read the same JSON and are checked against each other on a shared fixture; a divergence fails the
              Pages build.
          scripts/tests/test_roi_parity.py
