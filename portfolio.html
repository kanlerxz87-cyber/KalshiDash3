Portfolio performance & trade execution

Trade accounting is split into three separate, clearly labelled ledgers so a paper number is never mistaken for a demo-account number: (1) real demo account — empty until keys exist; (2) executor — a working script that posts real demo orders the moment keys are supplied; (3) paper ledger — a local simulation so the analyse → categorise → strategy loop is testable now.

Why there is no demo-account balance on this page. A Kalshi demo account must be created by a person at demo.kalshi.co/sign-up, and Kalshi states it “does not come with funds preloaded” (Kalshi Help Center). API keys do not exist until then. Printing a balance without a key would be fabrication, so the cell stays blank and is listed on Flagged for Review (F-07). This is the one step that cannot be automated.

2 · Executor — ready, unexercised

scripts/kalshi_client.py signs requests with Ed25519 or RSA-PSS exactly as documented (timestamp + method + path without query, 30-second clock tolerance) and posts to the current V2 endpoint POST /portfolio/events/orders. It reads credentials only from environment variables, never from the repo.

3 · Paper ledger — the analysis loop, testable now

Stored only in this browser (localStorage), starting at $10,000 of pretend cash. It is a simulation and is labelled as one everywhere. Fees are modelled at the documented general taker rate so the P&L is not flattering: fee = ceil(M × 0.07 × C × P × (1 − P)) (fee schedule). Per-series multipliers can differ, so treat net P&L as an estimate.

4 · Automated simulated trading — the strategies run themselves

The engine below replays the collected price history — real observations from the official demo API — through the strategy rules defined on Strategies, with no manual input: it decides every entry and exit, models the taker fee on every fill, categorises every trade by strategy, category and price band, and reports performance. Fills happen only at collected observation timestamps: entries at that observation's best ask, exits at its best bid. Nothing between observations is invented.

AUTOMATED PAPER SIMULATION — not a demo-account record, not advice. Every number in this section is computed in your browser from committed snapshots. No demo credentials exist (F-07), so nothing here touched the exchange. Per-series fee multipliers are not applied (the general rate is), fills assume zero slippage within the displayed depth, and the replay is a pure function of the current snapshot: a market that settles later resolves only on the run that first sees its result.
Engine ruleExact behaviour in this build
S1 momentum Universe: top 3 open markets per category by dollars-traded estimate with ≥2 collected observations (the selector widens this for exploration only). Enter YES at an observation's best ask when the price moved ≥5¢ between two consecutive observations and 24h-window volume grew. Exit when price retraces 5¢ from the position's peak, or hold to the end of collected history (marked to market).
S2 = S1 + liquidity floor Identical to S1, but an entry is only allowed when the snapshot order book shows ≥100 contracts resting at the best ask. Comparing S2 against S1 isolates what the liquidity filter is worth.
S3 = S1 + fee-aware band Identical to S1, but entries priced inside the 25–75¢ band (where the modelled fee is largest) are skipped unless the triggering move was ≥10¢.
S4 resolution fade At a market's final collected observation: if the price is ≤10¢ (or ≥90¢) and the favoured side has zero resting bid contracts in the snapshot book, take the faded side at ≤20¢. Held until the market settles; the payoff is the literal API result applied as $1 per winning contract, no settlement fee (Market Settlement).
Fees ceil(0.07 × C × P × (1−P) × 100) / 100 per fill — the general taker rate from the fee schedule, ceiling to the cent, applied on entry and exit. Identical to the manual ledger above.
Position size $100 of paper cash per entry at the observed fill price; $10,000 starting cash; YES side for S1–S3, either side for S4.
