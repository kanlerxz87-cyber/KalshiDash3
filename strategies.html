Trade categorisation & strategy candidates

None of these is a proven edge, and none is financial advice. Each one states the rule, the evidence that would be required before believing it, and how it fails. Every rule is expressed using quantities this site actually measures, so it can be tested against the collected snapshot rather than asserted.

Strategy candidates

IDRuleEvidence required before believing itHow it failsStatus
S1 Size-weighted momentum. Only consider the top 3 markets by dollars traded per category; enter with the trend when the last price moved more than 5¢ inside one collected window and volume grew. Resolved-market backtest over at least 30 markets per category, fee-adjusted, with the same window length used live. Chasing crowded markets into a reversal; slippage larger than the move; the window is one collector run, so a "move" can be an artefact of the sampling interval. untested
S2 Liquidity floor. Skip any market with fewer than 100 contracts resting at the best ask, regardless of how attractive the price is. Fill-rate comparison between filtered and unfiltered paper trades, recorded per trade. Excludes thin but genuinely mispriced markets; the depth snapshot goes stale between runs. untested
S3 Fee-aware band selection. Prefer bands where the modelled fee is below 1% of stake, i.e. avoid roughly 25–75¢ unless the expected move justifies it. Fee-as-%-of-stake measured from real fills (fee_cost in GET /portfolio/fills) rather than the general formula. Ignores that near-50¢ markets are where the most information is priced in; per-series multipliers can be 0/0, which destroys the premise. untested
S4 Resolution fade. Take the opposite side of a market priced below 10¢ or above 90¢ only when the order book is one-sided (no resting bids on the opposing side). Backtest against settled markets with the literal result field; report tail loss explicitly, since a binary pays all or nothing. Extreme prices are usually correct. This strategy's failure mode is a cluster of total losses, which a win-rate number hides. untested

What the collected data can already test

Nothing yet, and here is why. A strategy can only be tested against outcomes, and outcomes come from markets that have settled inside the five requested topics. The collector reports that count on the dashboard resolution tracker and on each category page. If it is zero, the sample is empty and any win rate would be invented. S1 additionally needs at least two price observations per market, which only accumulate over successive collector runs.

Automated test loop

These four rules are implemented as a deterministic automated paper engine on the Portfolio page (section 4), which replays collected price history through each rule with modelled fees and categorised results. That engine is a test loop over demo snapshots — running it does not satisfy the promotion rule above (no resolved-trade backtest, no fee-verified fills), so every row above stays “untested” until the evidence column is met.

Promotion rule

A strategy moves from untested to supported only when: (1) it has at least 30 categorised, resolved trades; (2) P&L is reported net of modelled fees; (3) the backtest sample is disjoint from the period the rule was invented on; and (4) the worst-case run of losses is stated alongside the win rate. Until then the honest label is “untested”, which is what every row above says.
